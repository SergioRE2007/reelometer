  • Reelometer counts the short videos you watch on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, using Android’s accessibility service.

01 Who’s behind it

Reelometer is an app developed by Sergio Rodríguez Espinosa, a private individual who makes it in his spare time. For any question about this policy or about your data, write to sergiore2007@gmail.com or open an issue at github.com/SergioRE2007/reelometer/issues ↗.

Since the app sends no data off your phone, we don’t receive, see or keep any information about you or about what you do with it.

02 What data the app handles, and why

2.1 What the accessibility service detects

If you turn on Reelometer’s accessibility service (after reading and accepting the explanation the app shows on a screen of its own), the app does the following while you use TikTok, Instagram or YouTube:

  • It detects when you move on to the next video in the short-video feed.
  • It reads that video’s visible text: the creator’s name, the description and the hashtags.
  • It detects when you tap a video’s “Share” button.
  • Only on Instagram and TikTok, and only if you turn on “Who you send to” (Settings › Apps; off by default): on those apps’ share sheet it reads the name of the person or group you pick (on TikTok, also the “Sent to…” notice that appears when you send, only to know it was sent; it isn’t stored), to show you who you send the most to. It doesn’t read the chat, the message you type or anything else in the conversation. On YouTube it never records who you send it to or where.

For each video, it stores on your phone:

The category is worked out on the phone itself, by comparing the description and hashtags against a list of keywords. No external service and no cloud AI is involved.

In other apps the service reads nothing: Android tells it you’ve switched apps and Reelometer only uses that signal to stop counting. It doesn’t record which other apps you use.

The service does not store what you type, doesn’t read your messages or passwords, doesn’t record the screen, and doesn’t tap anything for you or take screenshots, except with quick send (section 2.6): only with quick send, it crops the profile picture of the people or groups you pin from the share sheet, to show it on their button; it keeps it on this phone only and takes no other screenshot.

2.2 Your settings

The app stores your preferences on the phone: the daily goal, whether you want notifications and at what time (including the live alert when you pass your goal), which apps it counts, whether “Who you send to” and quick send are on, the names of the people or groups you pin for quick send (at most 3 per app) and where you leave their bubbles, the theme (light or dark), whether Passport mode is on, whether you’ve finished the intro and the guided tour, and whether you’ve accepted the accessibility service explanation.

To avoid repeating notifications, it also remembers the day of the last live alert and the last Passport level it told you about. Delete all forgets that too.

2.3 Passport mode

Passport mode (Flap, your traveler level, the stamps and the weekly challenge) is worked out on the phone itself from the history described in section 2.1. Almost everything is recalculated each time; apart from that, the app stores on the phone only what can’t be recalculated:

  • the date you earned each stamp and which celebrations you’ve already seen;
  • the highest level you’ve reached and the last state of Flap you saw;
  • weekly challenges already closed (the cap, that week’s videos and whether you met it);
  • the date you first shared a recap or saved a ticket (only that: not who with or where).

It’s stored in the app’s private storage, like everything else, it’s included in the CSV you export, and Delete all erases it. If you turn Passport mode off in Settings, it stops showing and stops notifying you.

2.4 Calibration tool (hidden)

Settings has a hidden technical tool, meant for tuning detection. If you unlock it and open it, it shows a log of technical events (which app, what kind of event, IDs and sizes of the on-screen elements) and, for each detected video, the creator, description and hashtags it read: the same data as in section 2.1, nothing more. That log exists only in the phone’s memory (the last 500 events at most), is never written to any file, disappears when you tap CLEAR or when Android closes the app, and only leaves the phone if you copy or share it.

2.5 What they aren’t used for

Your data is only used to show you your stats, your recaps, your passport, your notifications and the widget. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not passed on, and not used to profile anyone.

2.6 Quick send (Instagram and TikTok)

It’s off by default and can only be turned on with “Who you send to” on. If you turn it on in Settings and pin up to 3 people or groups from your “Who you send to” history in each app (Instagram and TikTok are kept separate), the app shows a button for each of them on top while you watch videos in that app. When you tap one, the accessibility service taps only these for you: “Share” on the video you’re watching, that person or group on the share sheet (if they’re not in view, it looks them up in the sheet’s search box; on TikTok, by tapping its magnifying glass first) and “Send”. It taps nothing else, never does it without you tapping the button, and if something doesn’t add up it stops and leaves the sheet open for you to finish.

To find the person or group, it reads the names shown on the share sheet (it doesn’t store them: it only compares them with the one you pinned). The send is logged in the “Who you send to” ranking. Pinned names are stored in your settings and Delete all forgets them too. None of this leaves the phone.

Profile pictures of pinned contacts. The buttons are bubbles with the profile picture of each pinned person or group, like the ones in Instagram and TikTok. The app has no internet and Android doesn’t give it the images, so, on Android 11 or later, the accessibility service takes a screenshot only when a pinned contact’s cell is in view on the Instagram or TikTok share sheet (when you use quick send, or when you share by hand with “Who you send to” on), crops only the circle of their picture out of it and throws the rest away immediately, without storing it. The cropped picture (a small image, 144 × 144 pixels) is stored only in the app’s private storage, is refreshed at most once a day, isn’t included in the CSV or any cloud backup, and is deleted when you unpin the contact or with Delete all. It takes no other screenshot. On earlier Android versions, or if the screenshot fails, the bubble shows initials.

A photo from your gallery. If you’d rather, in Settings › Quick send you can choose a photo from your gallery for each pinned contact using Android’s photo picker, which only gives the app the photo you choose (with no permission to see the rest). The app keeps only a small square of it (144 × 144 pixels), in its private storage: it doesn’t copy the original photo, it isn’t included in the CSV or any cloud backup, screenshots from the share sheet no longer replace it, and it’s deleted with Remove photo, when you unpin the contact or with Delete all.

To make sending faster, the app also remembers on the phone the technical IDs of the share button and of the sheet’s search box, how many times in a row each pinned contact hasn’t appeared in view (using a fingerprint of the name, not the name) and where you’ve left the bubbles. Delete all forgets that too.

03 Where it’s stored

In a database inside the app’s private storage, on your phone. Android stops other apps from reading it, and encrypts the device’s storage when you have a screen lock (PIN, pattern or password). We recommend keeping one on.

04 What gets sent, and to whom 0 bytes

Nothing. Reelometer doesn’t declare Android’s permission to use the internet, so it can’t connect to any server. It has no user accounts and includes no third-party analytics, advertising or crash-reporting tools.

Your data only leaves the phone when you decide:

  • Export CSV (Settings › Your data): the app creates a file with your history (including your “Who you send to” sends, if you use it) and your passport (stamps, challenges and level), and you choose where to save it or who to send it to.
  • Import CSV (Settings › Your data) goes the other way and sends nothing: with Android’s file picker you choose a CSV you exported earlier and the app reads only that file (with no permission to see any others) to restore your history and your passport. Settings don’t travel in the file.
  • Weekly backup (Settings › Your data), off by default: if you turn it on, you pick a folder on your phone with Android’s folder picker, and once a week the app saves the same CSV as “Export CSV” there (it keeps the last 4 copies and touches no other file). The app can only write to that folder; it isn’t a cloud backup and it sends nothing. If another app syncs that folder, that copy leaves the phone by your choice and under that app’s terms.
  • Sharing your recap: the app makes an image (a “ticket”) on the phone and Android lets you choose which app or person to share it with. It includes the figures for the period and, if there is one, the name of your most-watched creator.
  • Sharing your Passport level: same idea, an image made on the phone with your level, experience, completed challenges and the names of your latest stamps. No per-app video figures and no creators.

Once you share a file or an image, what happens to it depends on the app or person receiving it and on their own terms.

05 Backups and switching phones

Reelometer’s data is not included in Android’s cloud backup, and the app also excludes it from Android’s direct device-to-device transfer. A manufacturer’s tool might copy app data on its own; Reelometer isn’t involved and receives nothing. If you want to keep your history when you switch phones or after a reset, use Export CSV on the old phone and Import CSV on the new one (Settings › Your data). You keep the file wherever you like: if you leave it in a folder another app syncs (Google Drive, for example), that copy leaves the phone by your choice and under that app’s terms, not Reelometer’s.

07 How long it’s kept and how to delete it

The data stays on your phone until you delete it. You can:

  • Delete your whole history, passport included, in Settings › Your data › Delete all. Your settings stay, except the people or groups pinned for quick send, which are forgotten too.
  • Delete the settings as well from Android’s settings: Apps › Reelometer › Storage & cache › Clear storage (the names may vary a little by manufacturer).
  • Uninstall the app: Android deletes all its data.
  • Stop counting without deleting anything: turn the service off in Settings › Accessibility.

08 Children

Reelometer is not aimed at children under 13. Since the app doesn’t collect or send personal data, it doesn’t handle children’s data outside their own phone either. If you’re under 14 (the age from which, in Spain, you can consent to the processing of your data yourself), use the app with your parents’ or guardians’ knowledge.

09 Your rights

Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Spanish law, you have the right to access your data, rectify it, erase it, take it with you and object to its processing. Since your data is only on your phone, you can exercise these rights directly from the app: see it (Today, Stats, Recap, Passport), take it with you (Export CSV) and delete it (Delete all). The developer has no copy to look up, correct or delete.

If you have any questions, write to sergiore2007@gmail.com or open an issue at github.com/SergioRE2007/reelometer/issues ↗. You can also file a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (www.aepd.es ↗).

10 Other apps

Reelometer is not affiliated with TikTok, Instagram, YouTube or the companies that run them, and sends them nothing. When you use those apps, their own privacy policies apply. The brands mentioned belong to their owners.

11 Changes to this policy

If the way the app handles your data changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top, and explain it in the release notes for that version. If the app ever had to send data off your phone, we would ask you first, inside the app itself.
