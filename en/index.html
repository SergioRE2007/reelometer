04 Privacy 0 bytes

What you watch stays here.

Reelometer has no permission to use the internet. Without it, Android won’t let the app open a single connection. That’s not a promise. That’s how Android works.

It reads the screen in TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, logs each video, how long it lasted and what it was about, and keeps it on your phone. Export it as a CSV or wipe it with one button. If anything leaves the phone, you took it out. Not even the developer sees it.

An app that knows exactly what you watch and tells no one. Odd, yes.

06 Complaints 7 questions

Ask before you grant anything.

What you’d ask before letting an app read your screen. Short answers, no small print.

No. 01Why does it need accessibility?

Because Android has no other way to tell an app you’ve moved on to the next video. The service only reads TikTok, Instagram and YouTube: for each video, the creator, the caption and the hashtags. It doesn’t read your messages, what you type or your passwords, and it reads nothing other apps show. If you turn on “Who you send to” (off by default), it also reads the name of whoever you send the video to.

No. 02Which apps does it count?

Three:  TikTok,  Instagram Reels and  YouTube Shorts. A video counts once it’s been on screen for at least 600 ms. If you don’t care about one of them, switch it off in Settings.

No. 03Does it drain the battery?

There’s no measured figure, and we’re not going to make one up. What we do know: the service only hears from those three apps and does nothing outside them. It doesn’t work in the background either, except for the daily and monthly notifications (you can turn them off in Settings), the weekly CSV backup if you turn it on, a midnight refresh of the widget if you’ve added it, and a check every hour that the service is still working, to tell you if Android has stopped it.

No. 04Does it cost anything?

No. No ads, no subscription, no “pro” version. It’s made by a student in their spare time. If it’s useful and you feel like it, you can buy them a coffee on Buy Me a Coffee ↗.

No. 05Why isn’t it on Google Play?

It isn’t yet. Meanwhile, the APK comes from GitHub, with its source code right next to it for anyone who wants a look. That’s why Android 13 shows the “Restricted setting”: it does that with any app that doesn’t come from Play. The steps are in the instructions on the back.

No. 06What happens to my data?

It stays on your phone. The app has no permission to use the internet: it can’t send anything anywhere, not even to whoever makes it. No accounts, no servers, no ads, no analytics. It only leaves if you take it out: by exporting the CSV, sharing your recap or, if you use the hidden calibration tool, copying or sharing its log. One button or uninstalling wipes it. The full version is in the privacy policy.

No. 07Does it block anything?

No. It doesn’t close apps, cut videos short, set timers or lecture you: it puts the scoreboard in front of you and the rest is up to you. If you set a daily goal, the most it’ll do is tell you when you pass it, and only if you turn that notification on.

This page has no cookies, no analytics and nothing to report. Neither does the app.
